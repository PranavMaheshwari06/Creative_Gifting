# Marketing Plan — Creative Gifting (written as a premium-wedding-market agency would)

> For the owner. Not an instruction file for the coding assistant.
> Prices, ad budgets and platform rules change: verify before spending money.

## 1. The core idea
In this market people do not buy "packing". They buy the feeling that their family's gesture looks
generous, tasteful and unforgettable in front of relatives. Our job is to make a stranger think
"I want my daughter's trousseau to look like that". Everything (Instagram, site, WhatsApp) shows
the finished work and the care behind it, and makes the next step (a WhatsApp message) effortless.

Referral and trust drive this segment. Strategy = look premium + be easy to reach + make happy
customers and wedding professionals your sales force.

## 2. Who we are talking to
1. The bride's / groom's mother and family (decision maker, 40-65, WhatsApp-first, Instagram-aware).
2. The bride or groom (25-35, finds inspiration on Instagram/Pinterest, shares with family).
3. Wedding planners, decorators, photographers, makeup artists, boutiques, jewellers (referrers).
4. NRIs arranging gifting for family in India (want reliability and photos as proof).
5. Corporate buyers: HR, admin, founders (festive gifting; orders start well before the festival).

## 3. Offer ladder (describe on site and WhatsApp catalogue)
Signature trousseau packing -> shagun trays and envelopes -> return-gift hampers -> newborn and baby
shower sets -> festive hampers -> corporate bulk gifting -> fully custom packaging.
Decide: "starting from" prices, minimum order, lead time, delivery area. Clear answers convert.

## 4. Start TODAY: Instagram (do not wait for the website)
Setup (this week):
- Business/Creator account, handle close to the brand name; same logo as the site.
- Bio formula: what you do + who for + city + proof + one action. Example shape:
  "Luxury trousseau and gift packing | Weddings - Shagun - Baby - Festive | [City] | DM or WhatsApp to book"
- Link: WhatsApp link now, website link once live.
- Highlights: Trousseau, Shagun, Baby, Festive, Corporate, Reviews, How to order.
- Pre-seed 9-12 strong posts before announcing, so the profile looks established.
- Turn on WhatsApp Business: catalogue, greeting message, away message, quick replies, labels.

Content pillars (rotate):
1. Finished work (hero shots, flat lays, details)
2. The craft (process reels: ribbons, folding, tray styling; satisfying close-ups)
3. Trust (reviews, delivery moments, real families with their permission)
4. Inspiration/education ("what goes into a trousseau tray", festival gifting ideas)
5. The founder's story (mom's journey; people buy from people)

Cadence: 4-5 posts a week (2 reels, 2 carousels/photos), daily stories, reply to every comment/DM
within hours. Quality beats frequency; consistency beats both.

Reel ideas (first 20 days):
1. Time-lapse of one full trousseau tray being packed 2. Before/after 3. "What's inside a Rs ___
shagun hamper" 4. Hands tying a signature ribbon bow 5. Unboxing from the receiver's view
6. 3 mistakes in trousseau packing 7. Mehendi/sangeet return-gift ideas 8. Meet the maker (mom)
9. Diwali hamper tour 10. A day in the studio.
Hook in the first 2 seconds; text on screen; save-worthy ideas; always end with "DM 'GIFT' to book".

Funnel: Instagram -> DM/WhatsApp -> quote -> advance -> delivery -> photo + review request -> repost
(with permission). The website supports this: portfolio link in bio, enquiry form, tracked WhatsApp clicks.

## 5. Google (the quiet money-maker)
- Claim and complete Google Business Profile: category, service area, hours, photos weekly, products,
  WhatsApp/phone, ask every happy customer for a review (share a direct review link).
- Website SEO targets: "wedding trousseau packing in [city]", "saree/lehenga packing [city]",
  "shagun tray decoration [city]", "return gift hampers [city]", "corporate Diwali gifting [city]",
  "baby shower gift packing [city]". One landing section per service, real photos, FAQ.

## 6. Other channels
- Wedding planners and vendors: offer a referral thank-you; give them a beautiful PDF/WhatsApp
  catalogue and a priority slot. Tag them in posts.
- Wedding directories and Pinterest: list portfolio, link to site. Verify current fees.
- WhatsApp broadcast lists (opt-in only) for festive reminders; Status updates daily.
- Offline: branded tags/cards in every package with the Instagram handle and QR to WhatsApp.
- Corporate: a short outreach list of local firms, a "festive gifting" PDF, outreach in August-September.

## 7. Timing (as of October 2026)
Peak wedding season starts around now and runs through winter; Diwali is in early November (check the
exact date) and corporate gifting is largely decided already. So: Instagram and WhatsApp Business first
(this week), a one-page holding website within days, the full site in the following weeks, and a
real push for the Spring wedding bookings and next year's corporate festive season.

## 8. 90-day plan
Weeks 1-2: Instagram live + 12 seed posts; WhatsApp Business; Google Business Profile; gather assets;
holding page with WhatsApp button; collect 5+ reviews with permission.
Weeks 3-6: Site build (see TASKS.md); 4-5 posts/week; reach out to 10 planners/vendors; first paid
test later in this window (small budget, targeted to city + age 25-55 + wedding/home-decor interests;
promote best organic reels; measure cost per enquiry).
Weeks 7-10: Site launch; announce; update bio/GBP links; ask all past customers for reviews/photos;
add testimonials; start monthly "behind the scenes" series.
Weeks 11-13: Review analytics; double down on top 3 posts' themes; plan spring wedding and next festive
season campaigns; build corporate gifting PDF; consider Pinterest and directories.

## 9. Numbers that matter (ignore vanity)
Enquiries per week (form + WhatsApp + calls) and where they came from; enquiry -> booking rate;
response time; reviews gained; saves/shares on reels; website: Lighthouse, WhatsApp-click rate.
Keep a simple sheet: date, source, name, occasion, status, value.

## 10. Rules of the road
Ask written permission before showing a customer's name, face or home. Never use fake reviews or stock
photos of work you did not do. Reply politely and fast. Keep a privacy policy and ask consent on forms.
